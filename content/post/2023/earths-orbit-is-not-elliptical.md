A couple of days ago, I had a discussion with a gentleman. It turned out to be interesting. But before I get into what I am going to talk about, I must say this. First, I respect people who seek answers. I might disagree, but that is no disrespect. I question statements. But those are only in the sense of challenging a view. I, like everyone else, do not know everything. And frankly, our observation of things around us is merely like blind men around an elephant. We each see only a small part of the reality. When a few of us look at something and put together our observations, we see what seems consistent. What is consistent is closer to the reality than what does not add up. What does not add up should be challenged, and the answers we get from that may take us a little closer to the reality.

With the philosophy bit aside, let me come to what this article is about. This began with the discussion. Let's call the gentleman "Uncle". Here is what he said:

Uncle: What is the shape of the orbit of earth?

Me: Ellipse.

Uncle: You are wrong.

Me: I see.

Uncle: Is the earth static?

Me: Of course not.

Uncle: What is it moving around?

Me: The sun, of course.

Uncle: Is the sun static?

Me: Of course not. (I see where you're going.)

Uncle: Then?

Me: If you look at it that way, yes, it's helical.

Uncle: Then you accept that you were wrong about it being elliptical.

Me: [Smiling], If someone asked me the shape of the orbit and I say "helical" straight away, would people not call me an idiot?

Uncle: No, why would they? Why should this not be taught to kids directly?

Me: That is because it's hard to see or imagine things directly in three dimensions. Two dimensional shapes are much easier to see and visualise. When we were in school, animation was not this accessible. It's difficult to explain this to kids.

And before I could say the path is not necessarily helical, he said kids are being lied to; that if told to them in the right way, they would understand it; that we had found this out millennia ago, and since the shape of the path is similar to the structure of the DNA, astrology is real---because these things are all related.

I am not going to get into the astrology part.

But I want to reintroduce something that we may have studied a few years ago. And that is:

## Frame of Reference.

Let us leave the definition aside for now. Let me give you an example.

You get into a train. The train is set to go to the west. You get into the western door of the coach. As you start to walk to your seat eastwards, the train starts to crawl westwards. At one point, to a person sitting outside, you seem to not move at all.

You settle down, but then, after a point, you feel bored. You bring out a ball and start bouncing it. To you, the ball looks to go down and come back up, a straight vertical line. But to the farmer outside, if he could see the ball, the ball would seem to be moving along a sine wave.

The train is moving at the speed of 120 km/h now. You throw the ball to a friend of yours, sitting a few seat rows ahead of you. You throw it with as much force as possible. And you know that you are capable of throwing the ball at 80 km/h at the most---the ball seems to go towards your friend with the speed of 80 km/h, but a kid out there playing in the field, if she could see the ball, would feel it's moving at the rate of 200 km/h.

All these three examples talk about the frame of reference.

The train, with reference to the station, is crawling at a certain speed. If the train could crawl smoothly, and you could not see outside, you would feel that you were walking within a static train, towards your seat. If you could look outside, and walk in the opposite side of the train's movement at the same speed as the train's movement, you would think that the station is stationary (because you are cancelling out the speed of the train by walking in the opposite direction, within the train, at the same speed as the train's). To the farmer who sees you bouncing the ball, he sees the ball move in the same direction as the train, along with the ball's up-and-down motion, whereas you only see its up-and-down movement, because it is coming straight back to your hand after bouncing off of the train's floor.

You see where this is going.

## The earth's orbit is not known

Yes. If you look at the earth's orbit from within the frame of reference of the solar system, the earth's orbit is elliptical. This is observable, and known very well.

But when you look at it with respect to the galaxy, and notice that the solar system itself is being dragged around the centre of it by the sun, the orbit may seem a little different: if the sun moves perpendicular to the plane of most planets' orbits, the path of the earth's orbit is a helix. If the sun moves in a direction parallel to that of the plane of most planets' orbits, the path is not a helix. But again, if you look at our galaxy cluster, then, the path is not helix, but a whole different shape. And then you take our supercluster into account, the path becomes more complex.

Again, you see where this is going.

In physics, this frame of reference is an extremely important aspect when you are making calculations. Without a frame of reference, vector calculations would fail. And without vectors, physics as we know it, would not exist.

And we still call the earth's orbit elliptical, because one would introduce unnecessary complications when explaining concepts like, say, eclipses, if we take into account all the movements of all the astronomical objects around us. We remove all the common factors first, before running calculations.

It is both, the fact and a lie if we called the earth's orbit elliptical, depending on how you see it. But for the most part, calling the earth's orbit around the sun elliptical is sufficient.
