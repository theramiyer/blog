A very counter-intuitive topic that is going to make me seem like a jerk in my community. Today I felt like talking about why it is normal to not respect "people who work on computers", and why I don't have any particular respect towards us either. That statement is broad, so let me narrow it down to exclude, like, farmers who watch YouTube videos on their smartphones: I do not have any particular respect towards my peers.

I have handled enough computers in my life so far to be authorised to talk about them, and to know what I am talking about. In my professional life, I deal with scripts on a regular basis. I deal with people who write PowerShell scripts as well. And believe me when I say that there have been times when I rewrote hundreds of lines of PowerShell code just because I was having a hard time reading and comprehending what a certain script was doing. And because the script used thirteen lines of code for what can be accomplished in one. One. Yes, you read it right.

> Quality means doing it right when no one is looking.  
> ---Henry Ford

During the internal review of my book, one of the reviewers was pleased with a statement I had made about saving milliseconds in a certain query that returned a PowerShell object. He also sent me the link to this post, called, [Software Disenchantment](http://tonsky.me/blog/disenchantment/).

> Smile when your friend says you took longer than 200&nbsp;ms to type out the three property names.  
> ---Ram Iyer, PowerShell Core for Linux Administrators Cookbook

On a processor that runs at 3.9&nbsp;GHz, 200 milliseconds is negligible---nobody realises this. But using 200 milliseconds for something unnecessary is _bad quality_. Not to mention, 200 milliseconds wasted on one query is five days wasted on a million.

And 200 milliseconds is on a 3.9&nbsp;GHz processor. Inferior processors would actually make you notice the lag. And that brings me to the next point, which I have always spoken about, but had no one to care about (just like this post): Better hardware only masks bad software. And this is a point Nikita has stressed to a great extent on his blog post as well.

Last week, I went to watch a film, where I saw an ad about some special edition of OnePlus&nbsp;6T (I don't pay much attention to ads). What caught my attention was that the phone has 10&nbsp;GB of RAM. My heart skipped a beat. And not in a good way. I mean, seriously, I have a bright-and-beautiful laptop that has 8&nbsp;GB of RAM. I code, I write, I work, I watch, I read, I create, sometimes play, and do all sorts of crazy things on it. It does not freeze on me on a normal day. The point is, I don't need more than 8&nbsp;GB of RAM for a normal day of mine. What the fuck does _a phone_ need 10&nbsp;GB of RAM for?

More importantly, why is no amount of RAM truly enough for an Android? Or Windows?

10&nbsp;GB of RAM on a phone for "speed" is downright pathetic.
