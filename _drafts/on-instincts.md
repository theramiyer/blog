Thinking is not necessarily a boon. It's perhaps a necessary evil that humans have to deal with. These complex thought patterns and chemical flow in the brains is almost like chaos sometimes. That is just thinking. Overthinking is at a different level altogether. And it is almost like a disease; you cannot control much of it (unless you learn to meditate---but that is a vicious circle itself). But something good does come out of it; once in a blue moon of sorts, you get this brilliant snap when all of your thoughts fit in and form a whole body of ideas, which are worthy enough of sharing.

The only challenge is putting it in words.

Human civilisation seems to have gone against instincts. Think about it:

1. We don't like truth (seriously), but we are taught not to lie.
2. We inherently get scared, but are taught not to be scared.
3. We have feelings; we're taught not to act on them.
4. We are inherently impulsive, but we are taught to think instead.
5. We are not designed to be loyal to one person (heck, it is against nature in some ways), but we are taught to be.
6. We are inherently mean and violent... you get the point.

I could go on. In all of these instances, we are going against what human nature is. We take pride in this: If we <do this>, what would be the difference between us and those animals out there?

Virtues. Virtues are when you go against your inherent "nature" and change to suit "the bigger good".

And when we "just be human" (I don't even know what that means, any more), we are somehow "inferior humans" (exactly).

Why all the trouble?

I have seen people who are virtuous, go by ethics, and all of that, suffer a lot more than those who don't. You have seen virtuous people suffer. And this is popular: to quote Basha (from the Tamil movie of the same name):

> Nallavangala aandavan naraiya sodhippaan, aana kai vida maattaan. Kettavangalukku aandavan naraiya kuduppaan, aana kai vittuduvaan.

That means, "God puts good people through a lot, but never leaves their side. He gives everything to the bad people, but doesn't stick by them."

Ridiculous, even if you assume that there's God.

Anyway. The point is, we humans go against our instincts and do what we were not even designed to. And for what?

Recently, I was listening to a podcast. The speaker was rambling on and on about a lot of things, and why he liked Starbucks. It was simple: no matter where he went, he got the same coffee that he liked. He said, 'Change fucks with your mind.' True: people like consistency. And why, even at the cost of mediocrity. There's a whole branch of mathematics to deal with this sort of calculations.

And then there's the fear of the unknown.

But again, is it intuitive to work according to the book? I mean, the "Where's the challenge?" question apart, does it do us any good? Sometimes, yes. Sometimes. The reason is, if you could only work on your instincts, you would be highly predictable. Yes, the other side of working by the book is also bad, but let's keep that aside for now.
